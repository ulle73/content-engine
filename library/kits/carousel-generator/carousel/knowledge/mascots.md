# 07 — Mascots (optional — and off-brand by default)

> **Note:** no mascot cutouts ship with this kit — the folder is empty by design. A mascot
> is optional, and only worth it if the style sheet calls for a character. Fill the middle with a real screenshot,
> a glass data card, or a dark/monochrome cutout instead. If the user does want a character,
> it should be a cool-toned silhouette or a glass/translucent form lit by teal rim light.

A recurring character makes a series recognizable. Keep the SAME character across
slides — only change the pose/prop. Make it your own (don't copy another brand's mascot).

## Base prompt (edit the color + pose)
```
3D isometric voxel illustration of a cute cubic mascot character built from small cubes,
[COLOR — e.g. bright indigo-blue body with a cream face panel and small yellow/orange accent cubes],
big friendly smiling face, [POSE], matte plastic toy finish, soft studio lighting,
playful LEGO-like aesthetic, full body, centered, isolated on a plain flat off-white studio background,
high detail, clean product render.
```
- Aspect ratio **3:4**.
- Match the **accent colors** of your style sheet.

## Poses (one per slide role)
- **Reveal / hook:** "both arms raised in an excited 'ta-da' gesture"
- **Point:** "one arm raised pointing forward, excited"
- **Money/price:** "holding a large shiny gold coin, thumbs up"
- **Fast:** "in a dynamic running/dashing pose with small motion streaks"
- **Curious/check:** "holding a large magnifying glass, examining"
- **CTA:** "holding a small book / waving hello"

## Make it float on the scene
1. Generate the mascot on a **plain background**.
2. **Remove the background** (remove.bg, or any background remover) → transparent PNG. 
3. Place it **big & centered** as a hero, OR **small in a bottom corner** next to a chart.
4. If your slide has a grain/film overlay, apply it over the mascot too — it blends the toy into the photo.

## Keep the data honest
If a mascot replaces a stat, put the real number in a small caption pill so nothing is lost.
